# The house voice

One person writes this site, and she is writing to one reader who has the yarn in her hand.
Everything below has been decided at least once, usually after it read wrong on the page.

## Words this site uses, and does not

| Say | Never | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| yarn notes | reviews | She writes down what a yarn did; she does not score it |
| yarn count, or count | mill count | A coinage; a reader who searches for it finds nothing |
| this pattern is free (in its licence block) | "free patterns" as a label | A promise printed on every page is expensive to walk back, and it reads cheap |
| Craft Yarn Council for correctness | CYC's own wording | Her call: the table is a reference, not a style guide |
| single crochet, dc, ch | UK terms | US crochet terms throughout — the one deliberate exception to the spelling below |

British spelling everywhere else: colour, metres, grams, recognise. (`color` in CSS is a
property, not prose.)

## How it reads

- **Short sentences, plain words, the useful thing first.** If a sentence needs a second
  clause to explain the first, it is two sentences.
- **Explain a term the first time it appears.** A ball-band reader does not know what Nm is.
- **No marketing adjectives** — nothing is stunning, effortless or perfect — and **no
  "simply", "just" or "obviously"**: they tell a stuck reader the fault is hers.
- **Never blame the reader.** No "invalid", no "you failed to". The page is what is confused.
- **Do not promise what the site does not do.** Charts, schematics and photo tutorials are
  the paid PDF; the page never implies one is coming.

## Messages a reader has to act on

- **Name the field, and say what to do about it**: "clear the metres box", not "check your
  input". A message that names no action sends someone hunting for a setting they cannot name.
- **A message does not reword itself while someone types.** It names the problem, without a
  running figure: "Waiting for the percentages to add up to 100", never "they come to 66% now".
- **Say which figure was used** when two disagree, and which one the answer came from.
- **Never colour alone**: a heading, a mark and a tint, with the words carrying the meaning.

## Figures

- `m/100 g`, never `m / 100 g`. Thousands carry a comma: 1,400.
- A number never parts from its unit at a line break — join them with no-break spaces.
- Ranges run low to high, and two bands never share an edge: 270–349, then 350–549.
- Round before you display, and display what the answer was worked out from.

## Alt text and metadata

- **Alt text describes what is visible, never what it means.** On a quiz question asking which
  fibre this is, alt text saying "mohair" reads the answer aloud to a screen-reader user.
- Meta description: under 160 characters, says what the page actually is, no tease.
- A photo that does not exist gets no placeholder prose. The block simply is not there.

## Naming something new

The site will have to keep saying it. Prefer the word the trade already uses (a weaver, a
cone seller, a machine knitter), check it is a real term before coining one, and once it is
chosen, use it everywhere — a second name for the same thing is a bug.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Borrowing the standard's phrasing because it is authoritative | The page stops sounding like her |
| Writing a message about the *state* ("Invalid length") | The reader learns nothing to do |
| Renaming a row or a label without migrating what it meant | Existing entries quietly start lying |
| Hedging with "simply" or "just" | Reads as blame to the one reader who is stuck |
| Announcing a feature the page does not have | The free page gives away the paid PDF |
