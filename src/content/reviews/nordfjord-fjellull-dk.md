## What that adds up to

A single ply has no plying twist to spring the fabric back, so once water relaxes the
stitches they stay relaxed: about 6% wider and 5% longer after blocking, and it keeps
moving under its own weight in anything long. That is easy to design around — swatch,
block, measure the blocked figure, and don't hang a heavy hem off it.

What you get in return is the best stitch definition I have measured in a rustic wool at
this price. I would use it for textured cushions, hats, mitts, baskets and yoke
colourwork; I would not use it for a long cardigan, next to skin, or anything going in a
machine. It splits a little, it sheds for the first ball, and it wants a blunt hook.
[The DK wool test](/journal/six-dk-wools-swatched-and-washed/) has the comparison
against four other wools.
