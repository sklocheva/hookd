The Öland is deliberately plain. It is a straight body, a set-in sleeve, and a band —
the kind of cardigan that gets worn rather than admired. The interest is in the fit,
which is why both gauges are stated and why the piece gauge is the one to match.

## Notes before the first round

Work the body in one piece to the underarm, then divide. The band is picked up along
the front edge at the end and worked with the smaller hook, which keeps it from
flaring — a band worked at body gauge will always ripple.

The turning chain of 2 does **not** count as a stitch anywhere in this pattern.

## Back

<Row label="Row 1" side="RS" counts={{ XS: 62, M: 79, '3X': 115 }}>
  Ch 2, hdc in the 3rd ch from hook and in each ch to end, turn.
</Row>

<Row label="Rows 2–4" counts={{ XS: 62, M: 79, '3X': 115 }}>
  Ch 2, hdc in each st to end, turn.
</Row>

<Row label="Row 5" side="RS" counts={{ XS: 64, M: 81, '3X': 117 }}>
  Ch 2, 2 hdc in first st, hdc to last st, 2 hdc in last st, turn.
</Row>

<Row label="Rows 6–38" counts={{ XS: 64, M: 81, '3X': 117 }}>
  Repeat Row 2, working the increase of Row 5 every 12th row a further three times.
</Row>

<Row label="Row 39" side="RS">
  Divide for the armhole. Work to the marked stitch, turn, and leave the remaining
  stitches unworked. Complete each shoulder separately.
</Row>

## Finishing

Block each piece to the finished measurements before seaming — the fabric relaxes
noticeably and seaming an unblocked piece will fight you. Seam with mattress stitch
through the outermost loop only.
