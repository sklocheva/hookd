    - label: Mattress stitch
      body: Place the two pieces right sides together, wrong sides facing out, and line them up — for row ends, match the bumps made by the turning chains. An oversized drop-shoulder cardigan in moss stitch, built the way a patchwork one looks like
it should be built and is not. There are no granny squares and no intarsia here. Each panel
is made of vertical strips — plain striped rectangles, the colour changed at the end of a row
— and adjacent strips stagger when they change. That is the whole trick: a checkerboard with
no mid-row colour juggling anywhere in it.

The strips are seamed along their long edges into panels, which means the colourwork is a
layer sitting on top of ordinary drop-shoulder construction rather than a new system to
learn. The sleeves have no blocks at all. They are picked up around the armhole and worked
down in the round, which is what turns the taper into an ordinary in-the-round decrease
instead of a row of trapezoids.

The gauge below was measured on washed pieces rather than on a swatch, which matters more
than usual here: a block gains about 9% across and loses about 2% up in the wash, and with
sixty of them the difference compounds into a whole size.
