If you have some non-superwash wool yarn lying around, you can turn it into a simple cat toy ball in no time.

The finished ball is light, slightly bouncy and has the natural smell and texture of wool that many cats find interesting. Plus, you get a free non-synthetic cat toy, which in my opinion is much better (and safer) than the ones in store!

## **What you need**

- Non-superwash wool yarn
- A yarn needle
- A laundry bag

### **Make the ball**

Start winding the yarn tightly into a small ball. Keep winding in different directions so the ball stays roughly round and compact.

Once the ball is the size you want, cut the yarn and thread the end onto a yarn needle. Weave it through the ball several times in different directions to secure it. You don't need to make the end particularly neat, as the felting process will hold everything together.

I like making the balls fairly small and light rather than winding them into a large ball.

### **Felt it**

Put the finished ball into a laundry bag and wash it in the washing machine with a normal warm wash. I tend to put it with my normal laundry if the yarn isn't a particularly strong color — we don't want it to stain our clothes.

The heat, water and agitation will felt the wool fibres together. The less processed the wool, the better felted the ball will be. When the wash is finished, remove the ball and leave it to dry completely.

If it isn't felted firmly enough, just try washing it again. Try a stronger cycle and add agitation. Make sure there are no threads left hanging, as the cat might pull on them and ingest them.

#### **A note about the yarn**

Use non-superwash wool for this. Superwash wool has been treated to resist felting, so it won't give you the same result. Choose the least processed wool in your stash — this will give the best result.

### **Why cats like them**

The finished balls are surprisingly light and have a little bounce when thrown or pushed across the floor. The felted wool also has a different smell and texture from the synthetic toys cats usually encounter. Once the wool gets wet, its smell gets stronger and attracts the cat even more.

#### **Safety matters**

Although all natural cat toys, in my opinion, are safer than synthetic ones sold in stores, they still come with risks. Make sure the ball is felted all the way and no threads are left hanging before giving it to your cats, as they can unravel the yarn, which poses a risk of choking or ingesting the threads. Always put the cat toys away when the cats are left alone at home, and let them play only under supervision.
