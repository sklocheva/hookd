Every yarn here was worked at 32 stitches and 24 rows of half double crochet on a
4 mm hook, by me, in one sitting, so the tension is as consistent as a human gets.
Each swatch was measured dry off the hook, again soaking wet, and once more after a
30 °C machine wash and a flat dry.

I did this because I have twice made a garment that fitted perfectly until the first
wash. Both times the ball band said the yarn was stable. Neither time was it.

Two of the six grew and did not come back: the Sandnes alpaca blend and the Karisma.
Five per cent does not sound like much until you put it on a 100 cm bust, where it is
five centimetres of ease you did not ask for.

The two that grew are also the two with the most fluid drape, which is not a
coincidence. Weight pulls; a yarn with no memory keeps the length it is pulled to.

## What I will do differently

Swatch gauge is not enough. A 10 × 10 cm square held flat on a table tells you almost
nothing about what a 60 cm long body panel does hanging off a shoulder. From now on I
measure a piece gauge too — taken on the actual garment, under its own weight — and I
publish both numbers on every pattern.

For the two that grew, the fix is not to avoid them. It is to swatch long, hang the
swatch for a day, and size down accordingly.
