A ball band carries perhaps a dozen numbers and only three of them are load-bearing.
The rest are either legally required, or a suggestion dressed up as a fact.

The three that matter are the **fibre content**, the **metreage per ball**, and the
**weight of the ball**. Everything else — the recommended hook, the stated gauge, the
CYC category symbol — is a starting point set by someone who is not you and who was
probably knitting, not crocheting.

Metreage per gram is the honest figure. A "DK" from one mill and a "DK" from another
can differ by thirty metres in a fifty gram ball, which is a different yarn wearing the
same label. Divide length by weight and compare that number instead of the category.

The recommended gauge on the band is almost always a knitting gauge. Crochet eats
appreciably more yarn than knitting for the same area — commonly a third more, more
still for dense textured stitches. If you buy to the band's yardage estimate for a
knitted garment of the same size, you will run out.

The CYC category is genuinely useful for one thing: telling you roughly what hook to
start swatching with. It is not a promise about drape, halo, or how the yarn behaves
wet. Only a swatch tells you that, and only a hung swatch tells you what it does under
its own weight.
