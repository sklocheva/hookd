## What is frogging?

Frogging simply means undoing your work, whether you're fixing a mistake or reclaiming the yarn. You work backwards, pulling the yarn out of the stitches one by one. The name comes from the little _rip it, rip it_ sound it makes — a silly little pun that just stuck.

## Frog, but don't make a mess!

One really important tip: wind the yarn back up as you frog. Leaving it loose will quickly result in a tangled mess that might take you hours to resolve.

If you have a yarn winder and a swift, this is the perfect time to use them. The swift holds the yarn as you unravel it, while the winder turns it back into a neat ball.

Don't have a winder? That's perfectly fine, too. Just make sure to wind the yarn into a ball as you go. You can even make a centre-pull ball by hand — [check out this guide here].

## Not all yarns are made equal

Some yarns are much easier to frog than others. I personally made this mistake a few times during my “I don't need to gauge swatch” phase. I've wasted really nice yarn because it either wouldn't frog at all or was so delicate that the yarn broke every other row.

If you take one thing from this blog post, let it be this: **be extra careful with fluffy and delicate yarns, or test the froggability of the yarn before committing to a large project.**

The problem is that when you frog, the yarn has to slide through the stitches and rub against itself repeatedly. With fuzzy yarns, the halo can catch on itself, making the yarn grip and tangle as you pull. Pulling harder can then make the fibres or plies separate and make the whole thing even harder to unravel.

As a rough rule of thumb:

**Smooth yarns:** generally frog well. Smooth, tightly spun yarns tend to be the most forgiving, including many superwash yarns and plant fibres such as cotton and linen.

**Fuzzy yarns:** tedious to frog. Soft, loosely spun yarns with a lot of halo can catch on themselves and require considerably more patience.

**Very delicate or highly fuzzy yarns:** potentially a nightmare. Mohair is a good example. Its fluffy and delicate structure can make frogging extremely difficult, and even if you manage to reclaim it, the yarn may not look quite the same afterwards.

Hard-to-frog yarns don't mean you shouldn't use them. Just know what you're getting into — and maybe don't make your first project with them something you're likely to frog five times.

## The weird noodle problem

If you notice a mistake and frog a section of your piece right away, the yarn will probably still look much like it did before you crocheted it. But if you frog a finished piece, you may notice that the yarn doesn't quite behave the same anymore. To put it plainly, it starts looking like a pile of curly noodles.

This is because yarn gradually takes on the shape of the stitches it sits in. The longer it stays in that shape, the more pronounced those bends and kinks can become. Washing and blocking can set this shape even further.

A little crinkle usually isn't a problem. But if the yarn is very curly, it can change your tension and the way your stitches look when you work with it again.

## How to relax frogged yarn

If your frogged yarn is very curly, there are a couple of ways to relax it before using it again.

The simplest method is to wind the yarn into a **hank**, rather than a ball, and soak it in warm — not hot — water. Once it has dried completely, much of the crimp should have relaxed and the yarn should be easier to work with again.

If you're in a hurry, you can also lightly steam the hank. Just be very careful, especially with animal fibres and acrylics, as too much heat can permanently change some fibres.

And make sure to wind the yarn into a **hank**, not a ball, before wetting or steaming it. A ball won't allow the water or heat to reach the yarn evenly, and a soaked ball can take forever to dry.

Once you're done, swatch again before starting your new project, especially if the yarn has been washed or heat-treated. Reclaimed yarn can behave differently from the original skein.

## Not every project needs to be saved

Sometimes you make a mistake that ruins the whole piece. Sometimes you finish something and realise you simply don't like it or never wear it.

Even if you spent 20 hours on something you hate, keeping it in the back of your wardrobe won't give those hours back. The best thing you can do is learn from what went wrong, reclaim the yarn, and give it a chance to become something beautiful next time.

Sometimes, the project isn't worth saving. The yarn is.
